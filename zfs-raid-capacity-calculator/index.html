      Growing the pool later

      • Add a vdev. zpool add appends another vdev and new writes spread across all of them, but existing data is not rebalanced. Give it the same redundancy as the others: zpool add warns about a mismatch, and a pool with a RAIDZ vdev can never remove a vdev again. Try zpool add -n first.
      • Bigger disks. Replace the disks of one vdev one at a time and let each resilver finish. The extra space appears only after every disk in that vdev is replaced (with autoexpand=on or zpool online -e).
      • RAIDZ expansion (OpenZFS 2.3 and later, raidz_expansion feature). zpool attach pool raidz2-0 newdisk adds one disk to an existing RAIDZ vdev. Parity stays the same (a RAIDZ2 stays a RAIDZ2). Blocks written before the expansion keep their old data-to-parity ratio until they are rewritten, and ZFS keeps converting space with the original width's ratio, so zfs list and df show a little less space than a pool created at the new width.
      • Mirrors. zpool attach turns a single disk into a 2-way mirror or a 2-way mirror into a 3-way one. That adds redundancy, not space.

      What the math assumes

      • The space accounting is ported from the OpenZFS 2.4.4 source: the partition table zpool create writes on a whole disk under Linux (1 MiB at the start, an 8 MiB reserved partition at the end, 512-byte logical sectors), the four vdev labels and boot area (4.5 MiB per disk), metaslab sizing (the space after the last whole metaslab is never used), RAIDZ parity and padding per block, and slop space.
      • Every vdev has the same type and width, and each disk counts only up to the smallest disk in its vdev.
      • No compression and no deduplication: files are incompressible and stored in full records of the chosen size. With the default compression=on, compressible data takes less space; small files and partial records take more on RAIDZ.
      • Slop space is 1/32 of the pool (spa_slop_shift=5), at least 128 MiB (but no more than half the pool) and at most 128 GiB. ZFS keeps it free for its own use, so normal writes stop when only the slop space is left. Each vdev with more than 64 metaslabs also sets one metaslab aside for the intent log; ZFS subtracts that from the slop space, so on most pools the two together still come to 1/32 of the pool or 128 GiB.
      • Not modelled, so real free space is lower: file system metadata (dnodes, directories, indirect blocks; at 128K records the block pointers alone take about 0.2% before compression, because the default redundant_metadata=all stores them twice), snapshots, reservations, special, log and cache devices, dRAID, and any swap or other partitions a NAS system creates on the disks itself.
      • zpool list and zfs list print binary units: their T means TiB. zfs list always converts space with the 128K block ratio of each vdev, so at other record sizes it shows a little more or less than the data you actually stored.
      • The result is only as exact as the disk sizes you enter, and the round number on a disk label is not always its exact size; for an exact figure, enter the size in bytes from lsblk -b or the disk's data sheet. On an empty pool, the USED + AVAIL that zfs list prints is shown under the results, apart from the metadata ZFS writes when it creates the pool.
      • "May fail" counts whole-disk failures only. It says nothing about the chance of a failure or about read errors during a rebuild.
