Homelab Power Cost Calculator

List the machines that run in your homelab, type the watts you measured at the wall and the price you pay per kWh, and see the energy and cost per day, month and year for each device and in total. Works with a single rate or a day/night tariff, and shows how long a lower-power replacement takes to pay for itself.

        What the math assumes

        • A year has 365 days (8,760 hours); leap days are ignored. A month is 365 ÷ 12 = 30.42 days, an average month, so a monthly figure will not match a 28- or 31-day bill exactly.
        • Average draw while on = idle W + (load W − idle W) × share of time at load. The time at load is spread evenly over the time the device runs.
        • A device set to hours per day or a time window draws nothing for the rest of the day. If it sleeps instead of switching off, add its sleep draw as a second row for the remaining hours.
        • Every day is the same: the cheap window applies every day of the week, and weekend, holiday and seasonal rates are not modelled. Times are local clock time; daylight-saving changes are ignored.
        • With a day/night tariff, a device set to hours per day is assumed to run spread evenly over the day, so the cheap window's share of the day (8 h of 24 is 33.3%) is also its share of energy at the cheap rate. Use a time window when you know when it runs.
        • The watts are what each device draws at the wall, power supply losses included. Nothing is added for a UPS; if your gear runs through one, measure at the UPS input or add the UPS as its own row.
        • The fixed charge goes into the totals only. It is not split across devices and does not change their share.
        • Payback: saving = (old W − new W) × hours a day × 365 ÷ 1,000 × price per kWh. Break-even = (new machine price − money back for the old one) ÷ saving a month. Price changes, interest and how long the machines last are not considered.
        • Everything is computed unrounded and rounded only for display, so rows may differ from the total in the last digit.
