    Gap: empty spectrum between the Zigbee channel edge and the nearest Wi-Fi channel edge; a negative gap means the Zigbee channel sits inside the Wi-Fi channel. ×2 means two Wi-Fi networks share the worst grade.

    How the grades work

    Each Zigbee channel is checked against every Wi-Fi network you entered, and the worst result counts. A Zigbee (IEEE 802.15.4) channel is its centre ± 1 MHz. A Wi-Fi channel is its centre ± 10 MHz at 20 MHz, ± 20 MHz at 40 MHz, and ± 11 MHz for 802.11b on channel 14.

    • Overlapped — the Zigbee channel reaches into the Wi-Fi channel (gap below 0 MHz). There the 802.11n mask allows 0 to −20 dBr.
    • Marginal — outside the Wi-Fi channel, but the near Zigbee edge is closer than one channel width to the Wi-Fi centre: 20 MHz for 20 MHz Wi-Fi, 40 MHz for 40 MHz, 22 MHz for channel 14. That is the mask's first skirt, −20 to −28 dBr (−30 dBr for 802.11b). Gap 0 to under 10 MHz, under 20 MHz at 40 MHz, under 11 MHz on channel 14.
    • Clear — the near Zigbee edge is at least one channel width from the Wi-Fi centre, past the −28 dBr point (802.11b: the −50 dBr zone). Gap of 10 MHz or more, 20 MHz or more at 40 MHz, 11 MHz or more on channel 14.

    These three bands are this tool's own reading of the IEEE 802.11 transmit masks; no standard defines a "clear" Zigbee channel. The 802.11a/g, 802.11n and 802.11ax masks for 20 MHz all reach −28 dBr at 20 MHz from the centre. Silicon Labs' measurements with a 20 MHz 802.11n blocker fall on the same lines: Zigbee 12 behaved as co-channel, 15 as adjacent and 17–26 as far away, and its radio tolerated up to 20 dB stronger Wi-Fi far away than adjacent.

    How the pick is chosen

    1. Best grade against every Wi-Fi network you entered.
    2. Fewest Wi-Fi networks at that grade.
    3. Channels 11, 15, 20 and 25 first: Zigbee2MQTT recommends these ZLL channels, ZHA recommends 15, 20 and 25.
    4. In the US, channels 25 and 26 after the others (reduced transmit power).
    5. The widest gap to the nearest Wi-Fi edge, then the lowest channel number.

    Channel 26 is graded but left out of the Zigbee pick unless you tick the box above. The Thread channel is never picked for Zigbee. The Thread suggestion uses the same rules without step 3, allows 26, and avoids the Zigbee pick and your current Zigbee channel.

    Good to know
    
      
      
        
    • Channel 26 is not universal. Digi lists its XBee-PRO S2B/S2C modules as channels 11–25 only, and ZHA's docs note that channel support depends on the age of the hardware and firmware and on the device's power rating. That is why the pick skips 26 unless you tick the box.
      • 
        
    • US: 25 and 26 at reduced power. Silicon Labs states that Zigbee channels 25 and 26 require reduced transmit power levels to meet FCC requirements (North America only), so they reach less far. Equally graded channels are ranked ahead of them.
      • 
        
    • Thread and Matter over Thread use the same channels. Thread runs on IEEE 802.15.4 channels 11–26, just like Zigbee. With two radios, give the two networks different channels: on one channel both meshes listen before they transmit and share its airtime. Home Assistant ties them to one channel only with multiprotocol firmware, which it no longer recommends.
      • 
        
    • 40 MHz on your own access point.  uses 40 MHz in 2.4 GHz. Silicon Labs recommends 20 MHz Wi-Fi next to 802.15.4 radios; when 40 MHz is needed, put Wi-Fi and Zigbee at opposite ends of the band.
      • 
        
    • A mask is a limit, not a measurement. Real access points are usually quieter outside their channel than the mask allows, and how much Wi-Fi hurts depends on its strength and airtime. Silicon Labs measured that even on far-away channels Wi-Fi above about −40 dBm at the Zigbee radio (at 100% Wi-Fi airtime) reduces its sensitivity, so keep the coordinator away from the router.
