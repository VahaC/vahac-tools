Your domain

International domain names are converted to their xn-- form, which is what DNS and SPF use.

SPF: which servers may send

RFC 7208

SPF lists the servers allowed to send mail for a domain. Receivers check it against the envelope sender (the Return-Path domain), not the From address you see. Many sending services use their own Return-Path domain, so their mail passes SPF without anything in your record. A name may have only one SPF record, so every sender goes into the same one.

DMARC: what happens to mail that fails

RFC 9989

DMARC passes when SPF or DKIM passes for a domain that matches (aligns with) the From address. The record tells receivers what to do with mail that fails and where to send reports. It lives at _dmarc. in front of your domain.

Policy (p)
  1. 1 · Monitorp=none, at least a month of reports
  2. 2 · Quarantineas long again, compare results
  3. 3 · Rejectonly with DKIM on all mail

Relaxed alignment accepts subdomains of the same organizational domain (mail.example.com for example.com); strict needs the exact domain. Many sending services use a return-path subdomain, which only relaxed SPF alignment accepts. np covers names that do not exist in DNS, a common spoofing trick; it is new in RFC 9989 and older receivers ignore it.

Check SPF and DMARC records

Paste the TXT records you publish: raw values, quoted strings, dig or nslookup output, or zone-file lines. To count SPF lookups exactly, also paste the records of the domains you include, each under its name followed by a colon. This page does not query DNS, so it only knows what you paste.

Used for records pasted without a name, and to tell whether report addresses are on another domain.

Examples:
How to get your records 
dig +short TXT example.com
dig +short TXT _dmarc.example.com
nslookup -type=TXT example.com

Each line of dig output is one record; several quoted strings on one line belong to the same record and are joined without spaces. Run the same command for every name after include: and redirect= to count lookups exactly. Your DNS panel shows the same values.

How SPF lookups are counted

  • Counted: include, a, mx, ptr, exists and redirect, each 1, including the ones inside included records. More than 10 in total and receivers return permerror (RFC 7208 §4.6.4).
  • Not counted: all, ip4, ip6 and exp. Mechanisms after all are never evaluated, and redirect is ignored when the record has all.
  • Worst case: receivers stop at the first match, but mail from a server you did not list walks the whole record, so the full count is what matters.
  • Separate limits: one mx may resolve at most 10 mail-server names, and more than two lookups that find nothing (void lookups) also give permerror. Both depend on live DNS, which this page does not query.

Good to know

  • Mailbox providers expect all three. Gmail requires SPF or DKIM from every sender and SPF, DKIM and DMARC (p=none is enough) from anyone sending 5,000 or more messages a day to Gmail. Yahoo asks bulk senders for SPF, DKIM and DMARC at p=none or stricter, and Outlook.com has sent non-compliant mail from such senders to Junk since 5 May 2025. Small senders benefit as well.
  • DMARC changed in May 2026. RFC 9989 replaced RFC 7489. It removed pct, rf, ri and the !size suffix on report addresses, added np, t and psd, and finds the organizational domain with a DNS tree walk instead of the Public Suffix List. This tool follows RFC 9989 and points out what older receivers still read.
  • 255 characters per string. One TXT string holds at most 255 octets (RFC 1035). Longer records are split into several quoted strings, which receivers join without adding spaces. Most DNS panels do this for you; the zone-file line shows the split.
  • Organizational domain is a guess here. Whether a report address is on another domain is judged from the last two labels (three for names like example.co.uk). Receivers use the DNS tree walk of RFC 9989 §4.10, which can differ for unusual setups.
  • Not covered: DKIM keys, MTA-STS, BIMI and ARC are separate records. DMARC needs DKIM for p=reject, so set it up with your provider.
